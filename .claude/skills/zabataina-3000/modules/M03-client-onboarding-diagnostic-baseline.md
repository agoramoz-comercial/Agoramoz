## SECTION 001 — MODULE PURPOSE

This module operationalizes Module 01, Sections 007, 012 and 013.

No strategy, roadmap or execution plan may be produced for a client until this module has produced, at minimum, a **Minimum Viable Baseline** (Section 006).

The diagnostic must be fast, proportional and decision-oriented. Its purpose is not to collect information; its purpose is to locate economic value, the binding constraint and the feasibility of the financial targets.

---

## SECTION 002 — ONBOARDING SEQUENCE

The onboarding runs in four phases.

| Phase | Name | Objective | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Intent | Understand who the client is and what they want | 1 interaction |
| D2 | Baseline | Document the financial, commercial and operational current state | 1–3 interactions |
| D3 | Diagnosis | Identify the constraint, opportunities and feasibility | Analysis |
| D4 | Brief | Deliver the Executive Intelligence Brief and request a decision | 1 deliverable |

### 002.1 — D1: Intent contract

Establish, in the first interaction:

1. Organization name, sector, country/market.
2. Decision owner and budget owner.
3. What the business sells and to whom (one sentence).
4. Primary objective and definition of success in 180 days.
5. Selected financial track(s) — A Revenue, B Net Profit, C ARR, D Valuation (Module 01, Section 003.2).
6. Urgency and hard constraints (cash, time, legal, personal).
7. Available resources: capital, team hours, existing assets.

Record the result as the **Intent Contract**:

```
INTENT CONTRACT
Organization:
Decision owner:
Offer (one sentence):
Target customer:
Primary objective (180 days):
Financial track(s) and target(s):
Reporting currency:
Measurement start date:
Hard constraints:
Available capital:
Available team capacity (hours/week):
Definition of success:
Confirmed by client: YES / NO
```

### 002.2 — D2: Baseline collection

Collect the baseline through the Diagnostic Question Bank (Section 004), applying Module 02, Section 009 — maximum five questions per interaction, ranked by impact, with default assumptions offered.

Prefer documents over answers: financial statements, bank statements, sales exports, CRM data, analytics reports, price lists, pitch decks, org charts.

### 002.3 — D3: Diagnosis

Apply the Diagnostic Frameworks (Section 005) and Module 02's Z-REASON and Single Constraint Principle.

### 002.4 — D4: Executive Intelligence Brief

Deliver the brief defined in Module 01, Section 013, extended by Section 008 of this module.

## SECTION 003 — STAGE CLASSIFICATION

Classify the business using Module 01, Section 004.3, with the following evidence tests:

| Stage | Evidence test |
|---|---|
| 0 — Idea | No product delivered, no paying customer |
| 1 — Pre-revenue | Product or service exists; fewer than 3 paying customers or negligible revenue |
| 2 — Early revenue | Paying customers exist; sales depend on founder effort and are not repeatable |
| 3 — Validation | A repeatable acquisition channel or referral pattern exists; retention is observable |
| 4 — Growth | Defined sales and marketing processes; growth limited by optimization |
| 5 — Scaling | Demand exceeds delivery capacity or systems |
| 6 — Established | Stable operations, mature market position |
| 7 — Transformation | Restructuring, M&A, internationalization or reinvention |

### 003.1 — Stage-specific diagnostic focus

| Stage | Primary diagnostic question |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Is there a real, reachable customer willing to pay? |
| 2 | Can the first sales be repeated without the founder's heroics? |
| 3 | Which channel scales, and are unit economics positive? |
| 4 | Where does the funnel leak, and what is the binding constraint? |
| 5 | What breaks first as volume grows — delivery, cash, team or systems? |
| 6 | Where are efficiency gains and new growth vectors? |
| 7 | What structure, capital and sequence create the most enterprise value? |

## SECTION 004 — DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION BANK

Select questions from the bank based on stage, sector and gaps. Never ask the full bank.

### 004.1 — Financial

1. Revenue for the last 12 months, by month.
2. Gross margin by product or service line.
3. Monthly fixed costs (payroll, rent, software, other).
4. Net profit or loss for the last 12 months.
5. Cash in bank today and monthly net burn or surplus.
6. Debts, loans and payment obligations, with due dates.
7. Accounts receivable and average collection time.
8. Accounts payable and supplier terms.
9. Recurring revenue (contracts, subscriptions, retainers), if any.
10. Capital available for growth investment in the next 180 days.

### 004.2 — Commercial

1. Products/services, prices and share of revenue.
2. Number of active customers; top 10 customers' share of revenue.
3. Average transaction value and purchase frequency.
4. Lead sources and leads per month by source.
5. Conversion rates at each sales stage.
6. Sales cycle length.
7. Customer acquisition cost by channel (if measured).
8. Churn, repeat purchase and referral rates.
9. Why customers buy (in their words) and why lost deals are lost.
10. Main competitors and how customers compare them.

### 004.3 — Operational

1. Maximum delivery capacity per month with current resources.
2. Current utilization of that capacity.
3. Delivery time and quality issues (complaints, refunds, rework).
4. Team: roles, headcount, key-person dependencies.
5. Critical processes that are manual or undocumented.
6. Systems in use (accounting, CRM, e-commerce, ERP, communication).
7. Suppliers and supply risks.

### 004.4 — Strategic and governance

1. Ownership structure and partners.
2. Legal structure, licenses and regulatory obligations.
3. Intellectual property and proprietary assets.
4. Previous growth attempts and their results.
5. The owner's personal constraints and risk tolerance.

## SECTION 005 — DIAGNOSTIC FRAMEWORKS

Apply the frameworks relevant to the stage and problem.

### 005.1 — Revenue engine map

Map the flow from first contact to collected cash:

```
Reach → Leads → Qualified opportunities → Proposals → Customers → Delivered → Invoiced → Collected → Retained/Expanded
```

For each stage record: volume per month, conversion to next stage, time in stage, and evidence label.

The stage with the largest gap relative to what the target requires is the primary commercial constraint candidate.

### 005.2 — Financial health check

| Indicator | Formula | Warning signal |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | (Revenue − COGS) ÷ Revenue | Below sector norm or declining |
| Operating margin | Operating profit ÷ Revenue | Negative without a funded plan |
| Cash runway | Cash ÷ Monthly net burn | Below 3 months |
| Collection period (DSO) | Receivables ÷ Revenue × days | Rising or above terms |
| Customer concentration | Top customer revenue ÷ Total revenue | Above 25% |
| Debt service | Monthly debt payments ÷ Monthly gross profit | Above 30% |

Thresholds are default signals, not universal benchmarks. Adjust to sector and geography and disclose the adjustment.

### 005.3 — Capability assessment

Score each capability 0–4 (Module 01 scale):

| Capability | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Offer and value proposition | | |
| Demand generation | | |
| Sales conversion | | |
| Delivery and fulfillment | | |
| Customer retention | | |
| Financial management | | |
| Team and leadership | | |
| Systems and data | | |
| Governance and compliance | | |

### 005.4 — SWOT with evidence

A SWOT is valid only when each item carries an evidence label (Module 02, Section 005). Unlabeled SWOT items are opinions and must be marked [HYPOTHESIS].

### 005.5 — Constraint identification

Apply Module 02, Section 008. Declare one primary constraint and at most two secondary constraints.

## SECTION 006 — MINIMUM VIABLE BASELINE (MVB)

The diagnostic may proceed to the Brief once the following are documented, even if partially as labeled assumptions:

1. Stage classification.
2. Current monthly revenue (or confirmation of zero).
3. Gross margin estimate.
4. Cash position and runway.
5. Average transaction value.
6. Monthly customer or transaction volume.
7. Primary acquisition channel and its approximate conversion.
8. Maximum delivery capacity.
9. Selected financial track and target.
10. Primary constraint candidate.

If more than four of these items are [UNKNOWN], the Brief must state that diagnostic confidence is LOW and the first recommended action must be to close the most important gaps.

## SECTION 008 — EXTENDED EXECUTIVE INTELLIGENCE BRIEF

Extend the Module 01, Section 013 brief with:

1. **Feasibility verdict per financial track** — using the five classifications of Module 01, Section 003.3.
2. **Reverse-engineered requirement** — the number of customers, transactions or contracts required, per Module 01, Section 003.4, with labeled inputs.
3. **Primary constraint** — with evidence.
4. **Top three opportunities** — with Z-PRIORITY scores (Module 02, Section 007).
5. **Critical risks** — top three.
6. **Diagnostic confidence** — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW.
7. **Decision required** — typically: approve the target, adjust the target, or approve gap-closing work.

If the target is not feasible, the brief must propose a defensible alternative target (Module 01, Section 014).

---

## SECTION 009 — RED FLAGS REQUIRING IMMEDIATE ESCALATION

Escalate (Module 02, Section 010.4) before continuing growth work when the diagnostic finds:

1. Cash runway below 60 days without a credible plan.
2. Unpaid tax, payroll or statutory obligations.
3. Operation without required licenses in a regulated activity.
4. Negative gross margin on the core offer.
5. A single customer above 50% of revenue with no contract protection.
6. Evidence of misreported financial information.
7. Disputes between owners affecting decision authority.

Growth applied to a structurally broken business accelerates failure. Stabilize first.

## SECTION 010 — MODULE DIRECTIVE

Understand before advising.

Ask only what changes the decision.

Prefer documents to opinions and data to impressions.

Label everything.

Find the constraint.

Test the target honestly.

Deliver a brief that ends with a decision.

**NO DIAGNOSIS, NO STRATEGY.**
