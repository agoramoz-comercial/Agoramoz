## SECTION 001 — INTELLIGENCE MANDATE

Market intelligence exists to answer business decisions, not to fill reports.

Every research effort must begin with a **Research Question** tied to a decision:

```
DECISION: Should we enter segment X with offer Y at price Z?
RESEARCH QUESTIONS:
1. How many buyers in X have the problem Y solves?
2. What do they pay today for alternatives?
3. Who are the main competitors and how strong are they?
4. What barriers exist to reaching and selling to them?
```

Research without a decision attached must be challenged (Module 01, Section 001.1).

## SECTION 002 — RESEARCH PROTOCOL

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Define the decision and research questions |
| 2 | Define scope: geography, segment, period, currency |
| 3 | Identify sources by the hierarchy of Module 02, Section 005.3 |
| 4 | Collect evidence and record each source |
| 5 | Triangulate: confirm key figures with at least two independent methods or sources |
| 6 | Label and score confidence |
| 7 | Synthesize into decision-relevant findings |
| 8 | State limitations and remaining unknowns |

### 002.1 — Research integrity rules

1. Never invent market sizes, growth rates, competitor figures or quotes.
2. When no reliable data exists, build an estimate from labeled assumptions and state LOW confidence.
3. Distinguish global figures from local market figures. A global market size says little about a local opportunity.
4. Date every figure. Markets change.
5. When research tools are unavailable, say so and offer a structured estimate or a research plan for the client.

## SECTION 003 — MARKET SIZING

### 003.1 — Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| TAM | Total demand for the category in the defined geography |
| SAM | Portion of TAM the business can serve with its offer, channels and geography |
| SOM | Portion of SAM realistically capturable within the planning horizon |

For the 180-day mission, **SOM over 180 days** is the relevant number. TAM is context, not a target.

### 003.2 — Methods

**Bottom-up (preferred):**

```
SAM = Number of reachable target buyers × Annual purchase value per buyer
SOM(180d) = Buyers reachable in 180 days × Expected conversion × Value per buyer in period
```

**Top-down (for context):**

```
SAM = Category size × Share attributable to segment × Share in served geography
```

**Value-theory (new categories):**

```
SAM = Buyers with the problem × Economic value of solving it × Plausible price share of that value
```

When bottom-up and top-down differ by more than a factor of three, investigate before using either.

### 003.3 — SOM reality test

Compare SOM(180d) with the target from the Intent Contract. If the target requires more than a plausible share of SAM within 180 days, flag the target as high-risk or infeasible.

## SECTION 004 — INDUSTRY ANALYSIS

### 004.1 — Industry structure (five forces adapted)

| Force | Question | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | How many competitors, how aggressive, how differentiated? | Pricing power |
| Buyer power | How concentrated and price-sensitive are buyers? | Margin pressure |
| Supplier power | How dependent is the business on few suppliers? | Cost and supply risk |
| Substitutes | What else solves the problem, including doing nothing? | Demand ceiling |
| New entrants | How easy is it to enter? | Durability of advantage |

Rate each force LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH with evidence.

### 004.2 — Macro and environment scan (PESTEL)

Assess only the factors material to the decision: political, economic, social, technological, environmental and legal. Particular attention to: currency volatility, inflation, interest rates, purchasing power, payment infrastructure, import/export rules, sector regulation and labor law (Module 01, Section 004.1).

### 004.3 — Trend assessment

For each trend record: evidence, direction, speed, impact on the client (positive/negative), and time to impact. Ignore trends with no impact inside the strategic horizon unless they threaten survival.

## SECTION 005 — COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE

### 005.1 — Competitor identification

Identify three categories:

1. **Direct competitors** — same offer, same customer.
2. **Indirect competitors** — different offer, same problem.
3. **Status quo** — the customer's current workaround or inaction.

The status quo is frequently the strongest competitor. Never omit it.

### 005.2 — Competitor profile

```
COMPETITOR PROFILE
Name:
Type: direct / indirect / status quo
Offer and positioning:
Target customers:
Pricing (with source and date):
Channels:
Estimated size (with label):
Strengths:
Weaknesses:
Customer complaints (from reviews/interviews, with source):
Likely response to our moves:
Threat level: LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH
```

### 005.3 — Competitive matrix

Compare the client and key competitors on the 5–8 attributes that customers actually use to choose. Attribute selection must come from customer evidence (Module 05), not from internal opinion.

### 005.4 — Positioning gaps

Identify:

1. Underserved segments.
2. Unmet or poorly met needs.
3. Price-value gaps (overpriced incumbents, underpriced quality).
4. Channel gaps (customers not reached by current competitors).
5. Speed, service or trust gaps.

Each gap must be evaluated for size, accessibility and defensibility before being recommended.

### 005.5 — Ethical intelligence rules

Competitive intelligence must use only legal and ethical sources: public information, published prices, reviews, public filings, customer interviews, trade events and authorized research. Never recommend misrepresentation, theft of confidential information, unauthorized access or inducing breach of confidentiality.

## SECTION 008 — CONTINUOUS MONITORING

For active engagements, maintain a watchlist:

- Competitor price and offer changes.
- New entrants.
- Regulatory changes.
- Material macro movements (currency, rates, demand shocks).
- Customer sentiment shifts.

Report material changes through the escalation protocol when they affect an approved strategy.
