## SECTION 002 — SEGMENTATION

### 002.1 — Segmentation variables

| Type | B2B examples | B2C examples |
|---|---|---|
| Firmographic / demographic | Sector, size, revenue, region | Age, income, location, life stage |
| Needs-based | Problem severity, desired outcome | Goals, pains, aspirations |
| Behavioral | Buying process, current solution, usage | Purchase frequency, channel, loyalty |
| Economic | Budget, willingness to pay, value at stake | Price sensitivity, spending capacity |

Needs-based and economic variables are the most predictive. Demographics describe; needs explain.

### 002.2 — Segment attractiveness score

Score each segment 1–5:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Pain intensity | How severe and urgent is the problem? |
| Willingness to pay | Do they have budget and pay for alternatives today? |
| Accessibility | Can we reach them cheaply and quickly? |
| Size | Are there enough of them to meet the target? |
| Fit | Can we deliver the outcome better than alternatives? |
| Sales cycle | How fast do they decide? (5 = fastest) |

For the 180-day mission, prioritize segments with high pain, budget, accessibility and short sales cycles.

## SECTION 003 — CUSTOMER RESEARCH METHODS

| Method | Use | Minimum standard |
|---|---|---|
| Problem interviews | Discover pains and current behavior | 8–15 interviews per segment |
| Win/loss interviews | Learn why deals are won or lost | Recent deals, both outcomes |
| Customer data analysis | Identify best customers, patterns, churn | Transaction and CRM data |
| Surveys | Quantify validated hypotheses | Only after interviews |
| Review mining | Learn from competitors' customers | Public reviews, with sources |
| Pre-sales and smoke tests | Validate willingness to pay | Real commitment: deposit, LOI, pre-order |

### 003.1 — Interview rules

1. Ask about past behavior, not hypothetical future intentions.
2. Do not pitch during discovery.
3. Quantify the cost of the problem in the customer's terms.
4. Capture exact customer language for later messaging.
5. Record each interview with date, segment and key quotes.

### 003.2 — Evidence of demand hierarchy

From weakest to strongest:

1. Opinion ("I would buy that").
2. Stated interest (likes, sign-ups).
3. Time commitment (meeting, trial).
4. Reputational commitment (introduction, referral).
5. Financial commitment (deposit, pre-order, signed contract, payment).

Only levels 4–5 constitute validation for financial planning.

## SECTION 004 — JOBS, PAINS AND GAINS

For each priority segment, document:

- **Job to be done:** the progress the customer wants to make.
- **Pains:** obstacles, risks, costs and frustrations.
- **Gains:** outcomes and benefits desired.
- **Economic value:** the money saved, earned or protected by solving the job.

Rank pains by severity and frequency. The offer must address the top-ranked pains.

## SECTION 005 — VALUE PROPOSITION DESIGN

### 005.1 — Value proposition statement

```
For [segment]
who [job / pain],
[offer] is a [category]
that [key measurable outcome].
Unlike [main alternative],
we [key differentiator with proof].
```

### 005.2 — Value proposition tests

| Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Specificity | Names a segment and a measurable outcome |
| Relevance | Addresses a top-ranked pain from evidence |
| Differentiation | Clearly different from the main alternative |
| Credibility | Supported by proof (results, guarantees, demos, credentials) |
| Economic logic | Value delivered is a multiple of price |

### 005.3 — Value quantification

```
Customer economic value = (Revenue gained + Costs saved + Risk avoided) per period
Value-to-price ratio = Customer economic value ÷ Price
```

For B2B offers, a value-to-price ratio of several times the price makes the purchase easier to justify. State the ratio with labeled assumptions; never invent customer results.

## SECTION 007 — CUSTOMER VALUE ANALYSIS (EXISTING BUSINESSES)

1. Rank customers by revenue and gross profit.
2. Identify the top 20% and their shared characteristics.
3. Calculate retention and repeat rates by segment.
4. Identify unprofitable customers and segments.
5. Define the ICP from the best customers, not the average customer.
