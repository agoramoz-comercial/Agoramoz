## SECTION 001 — MANDATE

The offer and the price are the highest-leverage commercial variables. A small price or offer change can improve profit faster than any marketing campaign.

Offers and prices must be designed from customer evidence (Module 05) and validated against unit economics (Module 06).

## SECTION 002 — BUSINESS MODEL DESIGN

### 002.1 — Business model components

| Component | Question |
|---|---|
| Customer segments | Who do we serve? |
| Value proposition | What outcome do we deliver? |
| Channels | How do we reach and deliver? |
| Customer relationship | How do we acquire, keep and grow customers? |
| Revenue model | How and when do we get paid? |
| Key resources | What assets are essential? |
| Key activities | What must we do exceptionally well? |
| Key partners | Who helps us deliver or distribute? |
| Cost structure | What are the main costs and are they fixed or variable? |

### 002.2 — Revenue model options

| Model | Strength | Watch-out |
|---|---|---|
| One-time sale | Simple, fast cash | Needs constant new customers |
| Project / fixed fee | Good for services | Scope creep, uneven cash |
| Retainer / subscription | Recurring revenue, valuation | Churn, delivery consistency |
| Usage-based | Aligns price with value | Revenue volatility |
| Commission / marketplace | Scales without inventory | Two-sided acquisition |
| Licensing / franchising | Scales through partners | Quality control |
| Outcome-based | Strong value alignment | Measurement and collection risk |
| Hybrid (setup + recurring) | Upfront cash plus recurring | Complexity |

For the 180-day mission, prefer models that bring cash forward (setup fees, deposits, prepayments) while building recurring revenue where it fits.

### 002.3 — Model evaluation

Evaluate each candidate model on: cash timing, margin, scalability, defensibility, operational complexity and fit with the customer's buying behavior.

## SECTION 003 — OFFER ENGINEERING

### 003.1 — Offer components

```
OFFER DESIGN
Target segment:
Core outcome promised:
Deliverables (what the customer receives):
Delivery method and timeline:
Proof (results, demos, credentials, testimonials — real only):
Risk reversal (guarantee, trial, milestone payment):
Price and payment terms:
Bonuses / complements (only if they add real value):
Scarcity or urgency (only if genuine):
Call to action:
```

### 003.2 — Offer value drivers

Increase perceived value by:

1. Making the outcome more specific and measurable.
2. Increasing the probability of success (method, support, proof).
3. Reducing time to result.
4. Reducing customer effort and risk.

### 003.3 — Offer ladder

Design a sequence of offers that moves customers up in value:

| Level | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Low-risk first purchase, builds trust | Diagnostic, audit, starter package |
| Core | Main revenue offer | Full service, product, implementation |
| Premium | Highest value, highest margin | Done-for-you, priority, enterprise |
| Recurring | Ongoing value and retention | Support, maintenance, subscription |

Each level must be profitable on its own or be a deliberately priced acquisition step with measured conversion to the next level.

### 003.4 — Ethical offer rules

Never use false scarcity, fake deadlines, invented testimonials, hidden fees, misleading guarantees or claims that cannot be substantiated (Module 01, Section 010). Consumer protection law applies.

## SECTION 004 — PRICING STRATEGY

### 004.1 — Pricing methods

| Method | Basis | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Cost-plus | Cost + margin | Floor only; never the sole method |
| Competitive | Competitor prices | Reference for positioning |
| Value-based | Economic value to customer | Preferred for B2B and differentiated offers |
| Willingness-to-pay research | Customer evidence | Validation |

Price must lie between the cost floor and the customer value ceiling. Position within that range using competitive context and strategy.

### 004.2 — Price floor calculation

```
Minimum price = Variable cost per unit ÷ (1 − Target contribution margin %)
```

### 004.3 — Pricing structures

1. Tiered packages (good / better / best).
2. Anchoring with a premium option.
3. Setup fee plus recurring fee.
4. Volume or annual commitment discounts (with defined limits).
5. Bundles.
6. Local purchasing-power adjustment by geography (Module 01, Section 004.1).

### 004.4 — Price change protocol

When recommending a price change:

1. Calculate the volume loss that the new price can absorb while keeping gross profit constant:

```
Break-even volume change = −ΔP ÷ (CM% + ΔP)
where ΔP = price change % and CM% = current contribution margin %
```

Example: with a 40% contribution margin, a 10% price increase keeps gross profit constant even if volume falls by 20% (−0.10 ÷ 0.50).

2. Test on new customers or a segment first (Type 2 decision).
3. Communicate value, not just price, to existing customers.
4. Monitor conversion, churn and complaints.

### 004.5 — Discount control

Every discount must have a reason, a limit, an approver and a recorded effect on margin. Unmanaged discounting is a profit leak.

## SECTION 005 — OFFER AND PRICE VALIDATION

| Method | Evidence produced |
|---|---|
| Pre-sales / deposits | Strongest willingness-to-pay evidence |
| Landing page with real checkout | Conversion at price |
| Sales conversations with stated price | Objections and acceptance |
| A/B price test | Elasticity on comparable traffic |
| Van Westendorp or conjoint surveys | Directional price ranges |

Use the evidence hierarchy of Module 05, Section 003.2.
