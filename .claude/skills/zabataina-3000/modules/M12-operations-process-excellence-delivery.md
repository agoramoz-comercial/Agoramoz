## SECTION 002 — CAPACITY PLANNING

### 002.1 — Capacity calculation

```
Monthly capacity = Available productive hours (or machine time) ÷ Time required per unit delivered
Utilization = Units delivered ÷ Monthly capacity
Required capacity = Units required per month by the plan (Module 06) ÷ Target utilization
```

Default target utilization for planning: 75–85%. Above that, quality and lead times typically deteriorate.

### 002.2 — Capacity gap actions

| Option | Speed | Cost | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process improvement / waste removal | Fast | Low | Low |
| Automation (Module 13) | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| Outsourcing / subcontracting | Fast | Variable | Quality control |
| Overtime / temporary staff | Fast | Higher unit cost | Burnout, quality |
| Hiring (Module 14) | Slow | Fixed cost | Ramp-up |
| Capital investment (equipment, space) | Slow | High | Commitment |
| Demand shaping (pricing, scheduling, minimums) | Fast | Low | Possible lost sales |

Exhaust low-cost options before adding fixed cost.

## SECTION 003 — PROCESS MANAGEMENT

### 003.1 — Process mapping

Map core processes end to end: order-to-cash, lead-to-customer, delivery/fulfillment, procure-to-pay, hire-to-productive. For each step record: owner, inputs, outputs, time, cost, error rate and handoffs.

### 003.2 — Waste identification

Look for: waiting, rework, unnecessary steps, excess handoffs, overproduction, excess inventory, unnecessary movement, and unused talent.

### 003.3 — Improvement cycle

```
DEFINE → MEASURE → ANALYZE → IMPROVE → CONTROL
```

Define the problem with a metric, measure the baseline, find the root cause (with evidence; e.g. 5 Whys, Pareto analysis), implement and test the improvement, and put controls in place to sustain it.

### 003.4 — Standard operating procedures (SOPs)

Every critical, repeated process must have an SOP:

```
SOP
Process name:
Purpose:
Owner:
Trigger:
Steps (numbered, with responsible role):
Tools/systems:
Quality checks:
Output and acceptance criteria:
Time standard:
Escalation path:
Version and date:
```

Documented processes reduce key-person dependency, enable delegation and increase enterprise value.

## SECTION 004 — QUALITY MANAGEMENT

1. Define quality from the customer's perspective (Module 11 standards).
2. Build checks into the process, not only at the end.
3. Track defects, complaints, refunds and rework as KPIs.
4. Investigate recurring failures to root cause.

---

## SECTION 005 — SUPPLY CHAIN AND INVENTORY (WHERE APPLICABLE)

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Inventory turnover | COGS ÷ Average inventory |
| Days inventory outstanding | Average inventory ÷ COGS × 365 |
| Supplier on-time rate | On-time deliveries ÷ Total deliveries |
| Stock-out rate | Stock-out events ÷ Demand events |

Manage supplier concentration risk, lead times, currency exposure on imports and logistics constraints specific to the geography (Module 01, Section 004).

## SECTION 006 — OPERATIONAL READINESS FOR SCALE

Before Gate G3 (Module 08), confirm:

1. Capacity covers the base scenario plus a safety buffer.
2. Core processes are documented.
3. Quality metrics are stable.
4. Key-person dependencies have backups.
5. Systems can handle the volume (Module 13).
6. Suppliers can scale or alternatives exist.
