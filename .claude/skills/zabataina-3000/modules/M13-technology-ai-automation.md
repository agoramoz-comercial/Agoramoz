## SECTION 002 — TECHNOLOGY STACK ASSESSMENT

Assess the current stack across core business functions:

| Function | Typical systems |
|---|---|
| Finance | Accounting, invoicing, payments, banking |
| Sales | CRM, proposals, e-signature |
| Marketing | Website, email, ads, analytics, social |
| Commerce | E-commerce, POS, marketplaces, payment gateways |
| Operations | Project management, ERP, inventory, scheduling |
| Customer service | Helpdesk, chat, messaging platforms |
| Collaboration | Email, documents, communication |
| Data | Spreadsheets, databases, BI dashboards |

For each: current tool, users, cost, integration, data quality, pain points.

### 002.1 — Stack principles

1. Prefer simple, widely adopted, affordable tools appropriate to the company's stage.
2. Minimize the number of tools; integrate the ones that remain.
3. Ensure one source of truth for customers, transactions and finances.
4. Choose tools that support local payment methods, languages and regulations of the target markets.
5. Avoid custom development where a reliable standard tool exists.

## SECTION 003 — AUTOMATION

### 003.1 — Automation candidates

Prioritize processes that are frequent, rule-based, time-consuming, error-prone and stable.

Typical candidates: lead capture and routing, follow-up sequences, appointment booking, invoicing and reminders, onboarding emails, reporting, data entry between systems, inventory alerts.

### 003.2 — Automation business case

```
Annual hours saved = Frequency per year × Minutes saved per execution ÷ 60
Annual value = Hours saved × Fully loaded hourly cost + Error-cost reduction + Revenue effect
Payback = (Implementation cost) ÷ (Annual value − Annual running cost) × 12 months
```

Automate only after the process is standardized (Module 12). Automating a broken process produces errors faster.

## SECTION 004 — ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE IMPLEMENTATION

### 004.1 — AI use-case categories

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Content and communication | Drafting marketing copy, proposals, responses |
| Customer interaction | Chat assistants, FAQ, triage, qualification |
| Analysis | Summarizing data, classifying feedback, forecasting support |
| Research | Market and competitor research assistance |
| Operations | Document processing, extraction, scheduling |
| Software | Coding assistance, prototypes, internal tools |

### 004.2 — AI use-case evaluation

Score each use case on: business value, data availability, accuracy required, risk of error, privacy exposure, implementation effort and user adoption.

### 004.3 — AI governance rules

1. Keep humans in the loop for decisions affecting customers, money, legal positions or employment.
2. Do not feed confidential or personal data into tools without a lawful basis and appropriate contractual protection.
3. Disclose AI use to customers where required by law or where trust requires it.
4. Monitor output quality; define acceptable error rates and review samples.
5. Never present AI-generated content as verified fact without verification.
6. Document prompts, models, versions and data flows for critical use cases.

## SECTION 006 — IMPLEMENTATION PROTOCOL

```
REQUIREMENTS → SELECTION → DESIGN → BUILD/CONFIGURE → TEST → TRAIN → LAUNCH → MEASURE
```

1. Requirements written from the business problem.
2. Selection compares at least two options on cost, fit, integration and risk.
3. Build in a non-production environment where possible.
4. Test with real scenarios, including failure cases.
5. Train users; adoption determines value.
6. Launch requires approval when it changes customer-facing or production systems (Module 01, Section 009.2).
7. Measure against the business case.

ZABATAINA 3000 may generate code, configurations, prototypes and documentation as preparation. Deployment to production, purchase of subscriptions and changes to live systems require explicit approval.

## SECTION 007 — SECURITY BASELINE

1. Multi-factor authentication on critical accounts.
2. Role-based access; remove access promptly when people leave.
3. Regular backups and tested restore.
4. Secure handling of customer and payment data according to applicable standards.
5. Software and system updates applied.
6. Incident response contact and procedure.
