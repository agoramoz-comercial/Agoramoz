## SECTION 002 — BUSINESS-MODEL PLAYBOOKS

### 002.1 — Professional services and consulting

- **Key economics:** utilization, billable rate, project margin, proposal win rate.
- **Fastest levers:** raise prices, productize offers into fixed-scope packages, convert clients to retainers, sell to existing clients, referrals.
- **Common constraint:** founder delivery capacity; dependence on few clients.
- **$1M path pattern:** fewer, larger engagements (e.g., value-priced packages and retainers) rather than many small jobs.

### 002.2 — B2B SaaS and software

- **Key economics:** MRR/ARR, churn, NRR, CAC payback, activation.
- **Fastest levers:** annual prepayment, onboarding to activation, expansion pricing, focused ICP outbound.
- **Common constraint:** product-market fit or distribution.
- **$1M path pattern:** Track C (ARR) — model monthly MRR build including churn.

### 002.3 — E-commerce and direct-to-consumer

- **Key economics:** AOV, conversion rate, contribution margin after shipping and ads, repeat rate, contribution ROAS.
- **Fastest levers:** AOV (bundles, upsells), conversion rate, email/SMS to existing customers, best-selling SKU focus.
- **Common constraint:** paid acquisition cost; cash tied in inventory.
- **$1M path pattern:** high transaction volume — validate contribution margin per order before scaling ads.

### 002.4 — Retail, restaurants and local businesses

- **Key economics:** footfall, ticket size, visit frequency, gross margin, rent and labor as % of revenue.
- **Fastest levers:** pricing and menu/product engineering, local partnerships, delivery and catering channels, loyalty programs.
- **Common constraint:** physical capacity and location.
- **$1M path pattern:** multiple revenue streams per location, B2B/catering contracts, additional units only after unit profitability is proven.

### 002.5 — Manufacturing and industrial

- **Key economics:** capacity utilization, cost per unit, yield, working capital, distributor margins.
- **Fastest levers:** larger B2B/institutional contracts, pricing, product mix toward higher margin, reducing scrap.
- **Common constraint:** working capital and production capacity.
- **$1M path pattern:** contract-based volume with deposits and supplier terms to fund production.

### 002.6 — Distribution, trading and import/export

- **Key economics:** gross margin per unit, inventory turnover, credit terms, logistics cost, FX exposure.
- **Fastest levers:** exclusive supply agreements, faster inventory turns, negotiated terms on both sides, institutional buyers.
- **Common constraint:** cash conversion cycle.
- **$1M path pattern:** revenue is achievable; net profit (Track B) usually requires much larger volume — make this explicit.

### 002.7 — Construction, real estate and engineering services

- **Key economics:** project margin, backlog, retention payments, equipment utilization, payment delays.
- **Fastest levers:** milestone billing, tender pipeline, change-order discipline, maintenance contracts.
- **Common constraint:** cash flow and bonding/guarantees.
- **$1M path pattern:** a small number of contracts; manage collection risk carefully.

### 002.8 — Agriculture and agribusiness

- **Key economics:** yield per hectare, input costs, price at farm gate vs. processed, seasonality, offtake contracts.
- **Fastest levers:** offtake agreements, value addition (processing, packaging), aggregation, direct-to-buyer channels.
- **Common constraint:** seasonality, logistics, financing of inputs.
- **$1M path pattern:** aggregation and processing with secured buyers; plan around the season calendar.

### 002.9 — Education and training

- **Key economics:** enrollment, completion, price per learner, cohort economics, B2B training contracts.
- **Fastest levers:** corporate and institutional contracts, cohorts with pre-enrollment, online delivery to increase reach.
- **Common constraint:** credibility and accreditation; regulation of education services.

### 002.10 — Healthcare and regulated services

- **Key economics:** patient/client volume, reimbursement, compliance costs.
- **Mandatory:** regulatory and professional review before any change to clinical, safety or licensed activities (Module 16).

### 002.11 — Marketplaces and platforms

- **Key economics:** GMV, take rate, liquidity, supply and demand acquisition costs.
- **Fastest levers:** focus on one niche and geography until liquid; supply quality.
- **Note:** revenue is the take, not GMV. Never report GMV as revenue.

### 002.12 — Agencies and creative businesses

- **Key economics:** retainer share, utilization, client concentration, delivery margin.
- **Fastest levers:** specialization by sector, performance-linked or value pricing, retainers, productized services.

### 002.13 — Government and institutional contracting

- **Key economics:** tender win rate, contract size, payment cycles, compliance costs.
- **Fastest levers:** qualification and registration, partnerships with established contractors, focused tender selection.
- **Common constraint:** long cycles and delayed payments; plan cash accordingly. Strict anti-corruption compliance is mandatory.

## SECTION 004 — GEOGRAPHIC PLAYBOOKS

### 004.1 — Universal geographic checklist

For every market: purchasing power and price levels; dominant payment methods; dominant communication channels; trust and relationship norms; regulatory regime; tax regime; currency stability and convertibility; logistics and infrastructure; competitor landscape; language(s).

### 004.2 — Regional considerations (starting hypotheses)

| Region | Common considerations |
|---|---|
| North America | High competition and acquisition costs; strong digital channels; litigation and consumer protection awareness; state/provincial regulatory variation |
| Europe & UK | GDPR and strict data protection; multilingual markets; VAT regimes; strong consumer rights |
| Latin America | Relationship-driven sales; messaging-app commerce; installment payments; currency and inflation volatility in some markets |
| Africa | Mobile money and mobile-first commerce; messaging apps as primary channels; informal sector significance; FX availability and volatility; infrastructure and logistics constraints; strong role of trust and referrals; development finance and impact capital available in many sectors |
| Middle East | Relationship and trust-driven B2B; government and family-business buyers; local partnership requirements in some markets |
| Asia | Super-app ecosystems; marketplace dominance in e-commerce; highly varied regulatory regimes; price competition |
| Oceania | Small populations with high purchasing power; distance-driven logistics; export orientation |

These are generalizations for orientation only. Validate locally before acting.

### 004.3 — Lusophone markets note

For Portuguese-speaking markets (e.g., Mozambique, Angola, Brazil, Portugal, Cape Verde, Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé and Príncipe, Timor-Leste), communicate in Portuguese, respect local commercial and legal terminology, and verify the specific national rules on company registration, tax, labor, foreign exchange and data protection — they differ significantly between countries.

### 004.4 — Currency and purchasing power

1. Set the reporting currency in the Intent Contract (Module 03).
2. Convert at stated rates and dates; model FX scenarios where volatility is material.
3. When the US$1M target is expressed in a weaker local currency, show the local-currency equivalent and its sensitivity to FX movements.
4. Adjust prices to local purchasing power when selling across markets.
